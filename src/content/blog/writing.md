I've been sitting on this world for a while now, and I think it's finally time to start writing some of it down properly instead of just in scattered notebook pages and voice memos. This post is mostly a placeholder to make sure long-form, narrative-heavy posts render the way I want them to - headings, lists, images, quotes, all of it - before I actually start publishing real chapters or worldbuilding breakdowns.

## The Premise (Working Version)

The story follows a courier who delivers messages between rival city-states that haven't spoken directly to each other in three generations, using a network of old, half-forgotten roads that technically don't belong to anyone anymore. Along the way she starts running letters that aren't supposed to exist between people who aren't supposed to be talking.

A few threads I want to keep pulling on:

- What happens to trust between nations when the only communication left is unofficial
- A magic system tied to distance and memory rather than raw power
- Cities that have quietly diverged in language and law without ever formally splitting
- Whether "neutral ground" can survive being useful to everyone

Nothing here is locked in - this is very much still wet clay.

## A Character Nobody Asked For Yet

There's a minor character, a retired courier turned innkeeper, who exists mostly so the protagonist has someone to argue with about whether the old roads are worth the risk. He wasn't planned. He just showed up in a scene and refused to leave.

> "The roads don't forget who used them. People do. That's the whole problem." - Osran, probably not his real name

I don't know yet if he's important or if he's just a very persistent placeholder character.
